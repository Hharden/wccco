      <p>The cornerstone of West Coast survival is mastering the <strong>three-layer clothing system</strong>. Each layer has a specific job, and failing to coordinate them will result in either freezing from external water infiltration or soaking from internal sweat condensation. Understanding how these layers interact during high-intensity activities like biking to work or ascending a muddy ridge line is crucial for staying comfortable.</p>
      <p>Furthermore, consider the frequency of your exposure. Urban commuters who step from a car or bus into an office building face different saturation risks than backcountry backpackers enduring eight straight hours of horizontal coastal mist.</p>

  <span className="text-xs font-bold tracking-widest text-emerald-700 uppercase">Common Questions</span>
  <h2 id="faq" className="text-2xl font-bold text-slate-900 mt-1 mb-6">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
  
  <div className="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-4">
    <div className="bg-white p-5 rounded-xl border border-slate-200 shadow-xs">
      <h3 className="font-semibold text-slate-900 text-base mb-2">What hydrostatic head rating do men need for PNW rain?</h3>
      <p className="text-sm text-slate-600 leading-relaxed">For light city drizzle and short commutes, 5,000mm to 10,000mm is sufficient. For continuous downpours, high winds, or heavy hiking in wet brush, look for ratings of 15,000mm to 20,000mm+.</p>
    </div>

    <div className="bg-white p-5 rounded-xl border border-slate-200 shadow-xs">
      <h3 className="font-semibold text-slate-900 text-base mb-2">Is water-resistant the same as waterproof?</h3>
      <p className="text-sm text-slate-600 leading-relaxed">No. Water-resistant fabrics repel light moisture temporarily via a surface coating, whereas waterproof fabrics utilize physical membranes and fully sealed seams to block heavy water pressure entirely.</p>
    </div>

    <div className="bg-white p-5 rounded-xl border border-slate-200 shadow-xs">
      <h3 className="font-semibold text-slate-900 text-base mb-2">How do I maintain the waterproof DWR coating on my rain shell?</h3>
      <p className="text-sm text-slate-600 leading-relaxed">Wash your jacket with a specialized technical cleaner (like Nikwax Tech Wash) rather than standard laundry detergent, and tumble dry on low heat to reactivate the factory Durable Water Repellent finish.</p>
    </div>

    <div className="bg-white p-5 rounded-xl border border-slate-200 shadow-xs">
      <h3 className="font-semibold text-slate-900 text-base mb-2">Why do my clothes feel damp even when wearing a waterproof jacket?</h3>
      <p className="text-sm text-slate-600 leading-relaxed">This is usually caused by internal condensation (sweat) rather than a leaking shell. If your jacket lacks sufficient breathability ratings or pit zips, body heat turns into moisture trapped inside the garment.</p>
    </div>

    <div className="bg-white p-5 rounded-xl border border-slate-200 shadow-xs">
      <h3 className="font-semibold text-slate-900 text-base mb-2">Should I choose a 2.5-layer or 3-layer rain jacket?</h3>
      <p className="text-sm text-slate-600 leading-relaxed">2.5-layer jackets are lighter, more packable, and budget-friendly, making them ideal for casual travel. 3-layer jackets offer superior durability, comfort against the skin, and performance for heavy professional or backcountry use.</p>
    </div>

    <div className="bg-white p-5 rounded-xl border border-slate-200 shadow-xs">
      <h3 className="font-semibold text-slate-900 text-base mb-2">Are fleece mid-layers warm enough for coastal winters?</h3>
      <p className="text-sm text-slate-600 leading-relaxed">Yes, for most Pacific Northwest conditions where temperatures rarely plunge far below freezing. Pairing a middleweight fleece with a wind-blocking outer shell creates an ideal microclimate for coastal weather.</p>
